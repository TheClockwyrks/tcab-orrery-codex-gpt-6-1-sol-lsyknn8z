Orrery is an astronomical machine shop. Your raw materials are tiny celestial bodies: cold moons, bright suns, clouds of nebula, and sparks of nova. Your task is to turn them into the constellations an observatory requests. There is no countdown while you build. Take a moment to study the sky, imagine a path through it, and begin with a single arm.

Drag mechanisms and engraved sigils onto a hexagonal workbench. A rise brings new material into the field; a set waits for the exact product you promised. Arms reach across the sky, pistons stretch, and tracks carry entire mechanisms between stations. Sigils join motes with filaments, split their bonds, copy an essence, or climb the ladder of planets. Every placement is a small geometric decision, and even a modest collection of parts can suggest several different machines.

The rhythm is yours to write. Give each arm a tape of instructions: grab, turn, release, return. Every tape repeats on one shared period, so a second arm can meet the first at precisely the right moment. Start the machine and watch your plan become motion. Pause to inspect a difficult handoff, step through one cycle, or accelerate a reliable design. When motes collide, the sky freezes at the fault so you can understand the mistake and return to the bench.

Campaign offers sixteen workshops, moving from a first orbit through mechanisms, transmutation, and increasingly elaborate arrangements. Extras opens ten independent challenges immediately, from Twin Moons to the repeating Lunar Procession. Deliver every requested product six times to finish. Then keep tinkering: your best cost, cycle count, and occupied area are recorded separately. A solution is a beginning; a graceful solution is something you can keep pursuing.
